# Write useful skill instructions

Write as an experienced colleague helping someone perform the task. Explain the purpose and behavior before details unless the reader is looking up an exact interface. Assume the reader understands the field but has not seen the authoring conversation.

Understand the subject before choosing words. State what acts, what happens, and which conditions matter. Use familiar verbs and established technical names. Preserve exact identifiers, commands, labels, units, and meaningful exceptions.

Keep related ideas in connected paragraphs. Use lists for parallel choices or ordered actions, and tables for comparisons. Choose headings that identify the subject. Avoid slogans, invented labels, filler, repeated rules, condescension, and unsupported numerical targets.

Match the document to its job. Instructions explain what to do; a specification states expected behavior; a plan explains implementation; a report states observed results. Preserve certainty and obligation: a proposal has not happened, a symptom does not establish a cause, and “may” is not interchangeable with “must.”

Put each authoritative rule in one place. Keep discovery and essential routing in the entry point; load detailed guidance when relevant. Complete examples belong outside the entry point when their depth would obscure ordinary use. Do not replace important conditions with brevity.

Review meaning and evidence first, then language and flow, then repetition and preservation. Check headings, metadata, templates, examples, README instructions, and reports as well as the main prose. A clean wording scan does not establish understanding.

The approachable tone is consistent with [Google's voice and tone guidance](https://developers.google.com/style/tone).
